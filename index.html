Quantitative Aptitude

Mathematical concepts, formulas and solved examples.

1. Number System

Introduction

A number system is a method of representing numbers. It includes natural, whole, integer, rational, irrational and real numbers.

Types of Numbers

  • Natural: 1, 2, 3, 4...
  • Whole: 0, 1, 2, 3...
  • Integers: -3, -2, -1, 0, 1...
  • Prime: Numbers having exactly two factors.
  • Composite: Numbers having more than two factors.

Divisibility Rules

  • 2: Last digit must be even.
  • 3: Sum of digits divisible by 3.
  • 5: Last digit 0 or 5.
  • 9: Sum of digits divisible by 9.
  • 10: Last digit must be 0.
  • 11: Difference of alternate digit sums is divisible by 11.

Solved Example

Is 456 divisible by 3?

4 + 5 + 6 = 15. Since 15 is divisible by 3, 456 is divisible by 3.

2. Percentage

Definition

Percentage means a value expressed out of 100.

Percentage = (Value / Total) × 100
x% of y = (x × y) / 100

Important Conversions

  • 1/2 = 50%
  • 1/3 = 33.33%
  • 1/4 = 25%
  • 1/5 = 20%
  • 1/8 = 12.5%
  • 3/4 = 75%

Example

Find 25% of 800.

800 × 25 / 100 = 200.

Successive Percentage Change

Net change = a + b + ab/100

Use negative values for decreases.

3. Profit, Loss and Discount

Cost Price (CP): Purchase price.

Selling Price (SP): Selling price of an article.

Profit = SP - CP
Loss = CP - SP
Profit % = Profit / CP × 100
Loss % = Loss / CP × 100
Discount = Marked Price - Selling Price

Solved Example

CP = ₹500 and SP = ₹600.

Profit = 600 - 500 = ₹100.

Profit percentage = 100/500 × 100 = 20%.

4. Simple and Compound Interest

Simple Interest

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

P = Principal, R = Rate, T = Time.

Compound Interest

Amount = P(1 + R/100)T
CI = Amount - Principal

Example

Principal = ₹1,000, Rate = 10%, Time = 2 years.

SI = 1000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = ₹200.

Annual compound interest = ₹210.

5. Ratio and Proportion

Ratio compares two quantities of the same kind.

a : b = a / b

If a:b = c:d, then ad = bc.

Example

Divide ₹600 in the ratio 2:3.

Total parts = 5.

First share = 600 × 2/5 = ₹240.

Second share = 600 × 3/5 = ₹360.

6. Time and Work
One day's work = 1 / Total days

If A completes work in 10 days, A's one-day work is 1/10.

If B completes it in 15 days, B's one-day work is 1/15.

Combined work = 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6.

Therefore, both complete the work in 6 days.

7. Time, Speed and Distance
Speed = Distance / Time
Distance = Speed × Time
Time = Distance / Speed

km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18.

m/s to km/h: multiply by 18/5.

Example

A car covers 120 km in 2 hours.

Speed = 120/2 = 60 km/h.

8. Algebra, Geometry and Mensuration

Algebra Identities

(a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a-b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
a²-b² = (a-b)(a+b)

Geometry

Triangle angle sum = 180°.

Area of triangle = 1/2 × base × height.

Circle area = πr².

Circle circumference = 2πr.

Mensuration

Cube volume = a³.

Cuboid volume = l × b × h.

Cylinder volume = πr²h.

9. Trigonometry
sin θ = Perpendicular / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Base / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Perpendicular / Base

Important identity:

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

English Language

Grammar, vocabulary and comprehension.

1. Parts of Speech
  • Noun: Name of a person, place or thing.
  • Pronoun: Used instead of a noun.
  • Verb: Shows action or state.
  • Adjective: Describes a noun.
  • Adverb: Modifies a verb or adjective.
  • Preposition: Shows relationship.
  • Conjunction: Joins words or sentences.
  • Interjection: Expresses sudden emotion.

Example

She quickly completed her difficult work.

She = Pronoun; quickly = Adverb; completed = Verb; difficult = Adjective.

2. Tenses

There are three main tenses: Present, Past and Future.

Present Tense

Simple: I play.

Continuous: I am playing.

Perfect: I have played.

Perfect Continuous: I have been playing.

Past Tense

Simple: I played.

Continuous: I was playing.

Perfect: I had played.

Perfect Continuous: I had been playing.

Future Tense

Simple: I will play.

Continuous: I will be playing.

Perfect: I will have played.

Perfect Continuous: I will have been playing.

3. Articles

There are three articles: A, An and The.

A is used before a consonant sound: a book, a university.

An is used before a vowel sound: an apple, an hour.

The is used for a particular or specific noun.

4. Subject Verb Agreement

A singular subject generally takes a singular verb.

A plural subject generally takes a plural verb.

Incorrect: He go to school.

Correct: He goes to school.

Each, every, everyone and somebody generally take singular verbs.

5. Active and Passive Voice

Active voice: Subject performs the action.

Passive voice: Subject receives the action.

Active: Ram writes a letter.

Passive: A letter is written by Ram.

Basic passive structure: Object + be verb + V3 + by + subject.

6. Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct speech reports the exact words.

Indirect speech reports the meaning of the statement.

Direct: He said, "I am happy."

Indirect: He said that he was happy.

Pronouns, tense and time expressions may change according to context.

7. Vocabulary and Idioms

Synonym: A word with a similar meaning.

Antonym: A word with an opposite meaning.

Brief = Short.

Expand = Contract (antonyms).

Important Idioms

  • Once in a blue moon: Very rarely.
  • Break the ice: Start a conversation.
  • A piece of cake: Something very easy.
  • Spill the beans: Reveal a secret.
  • Hit the nail on the head: Say exactly the right thing.
8. Reading Comprehension and Cloze Test

Read the passage carefully and identify its central idea.

For comprehension, answer using the information provided in the passage.

For cloze tests, check grammar, context, vocabulary and sentence meaning before choosing an answer.

General Intelligence & Reasoning

Logical thinking and problem-solving concepts.

1. Analogy

Analogy means identifying a similar relationship between two pairs.

Example: Bird : Fly :: Fish : Swim.

Relationship: Animal and its movement.

Common patterns include function, category, opposite, part-whole and degree.

2. Number Series

Find the mathematical pattern in a sequence.

Example: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ?

Every number increases by 2.

Answer = 12.

Check differences, multiplication, division, squares and alternating patterns.

3. Coding and Decoding

Letters or numbers are transformed according to a particular rule.

Example: CAT → DBU.

C becomes D, A becomes B, T becomes U.

Therefore DOG → EPH.

4. Blood Relations

Questions based on family relationships.

  • Father's father = Grandfather.
  • Mother's mother = Grandmother.
  • Father's brother = Uncle.
  • Mother's sister = Aunt.
  • Brother's son = Nephew.
  • Sister's daughter = Niece.

Draw a family tree to solve complicated questions.

5. Direction Sense

Four main directions are North, South, East and West.

North is opposite South. East is opposite West.

Draw a direction diagram and trace every movement.

Distance = √(Horizontal² + Vertical²)
6. Syllogism

Syllogism uses statements to determine logically valid conclusions.

Common statements:

  • All A are B.
  • No A is B.
  • Some A are B.
  • Some A are not B.

Use Venn diagrams to check whether a conclusion necessarily follows.

Remember: All A are B does not mean all B are A.

7. Ranking and Seating Arrangement
Total = Left Rank + Right Rank - 1

Example: A person is 5th from left and 7th from right.

Total persons = 5 + 7 - 1 = 11.

For seating arrangement, draw positions and apply each clue one by one.
